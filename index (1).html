What happens to the string

One study counted about 4,800 metres of cut-out string at a single shop in a month.1 This is where ours goes instead.

  1. Cut

    The stringer cuts the old set out of the racquet.

  2. Bin

    It goes in our bin by the machine instead of the trash.

  3. Collect

    When the bin is nearly full, the stringer scans our QR code and one of us comes to get it.

  4. Clean and shred

    We take out any nylon or gut, wash the polyester and shred it. Learning this now

  5. Make

    Melt it down and form it into something new. Not yet

    Questions

    Does it cost the shop anything?

    No. We supply the bin and do the pickups.

    Who picks it up, and how often?

    One of us, the students running Relace. There's no fixed schedule. Each stringer gets a QR code from us, and when the bin is getting full they scan it and we come pick it up.

    What happens to the string right now?

    We store it, sort it and keep it clean. None of it has been made into a new product yet. We're learning small-scale mechanical recycling, which means shredding and melting with no chemicals, so we can do that part properly.

    Why only polyester?

    Most competitive players use it, so it's most of what gets cut out. It also breaks down very slowly in a landfill.2 Recycling works best with one type of plastic, so we keep it separate.

    What about nylon, multifilament or gut?

    Please leave those out for now. They're different materials and make recycling harder. If you're not sure, put it in and we'll sort it.

    Can players drop off their own string?

    There's no need. The shop cuts it out during the restring and it goes straight in the bin. The most useful thing a player can do is tell us about a shop that should have one.

    How much room does the bin take?

    It's 15 × 11 × 20 inches and sits next to the stringing machine.

    Can you help?

    We know tennis better than we know recycling. We'd like to hear from you if you:

    • work in plastic recycling or materials
    • coach, or run a club or team
    • have made and sold a physical product
    • know someone who does any of the above

    A note from the team

    Relace started as a project in our Entrepreneurship in Action class at the University of Minnesota. Some of us play, and we kept seeing the same thing at the shop: every restring ends with a handful of plastic in the trash.

    So we started putting bins out. We're a few students with a few bins, and we're still working out a lot of it. We haven't made anything from the string yet. That's the next step, and we'd rather get it right than rush it.

    If you run a shop, know one, or know something about recycling plastic, we'd love to hear from you!
